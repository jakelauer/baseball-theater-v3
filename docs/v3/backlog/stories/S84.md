**Gap:** A story's grader is meant to prove that story's work, but nothing keeps it to that. Some graders compare the whole repository at HEAD with the state before the story (`git diff <BASE> -- <path>`), so a later story that legitimately changes the same file turns an old grader red: S81 added Playwright to the root `package.json`, and `scripts/verify-S60.sh` check 7 has failed ever since, though S60's own commit (`74a93e0`) never touched that file. `scripts/audit.sh story <ID> --reverify` re-runs a grader against today's tree, and S81's pilot recorded the owner's S60 design verdict through such a re-run, so a design verdict was tied to an unrelated re-check. And there is no list of the requirements that must hold forever: the lines BT already guards in `pnpm verify` (`bt/no-browser-storage`, `bt/no-domain-arithmetic`, the personal-data manifest test, the route-table test, `pnpm size`, the coverage ratchet, rule 22 in `pnpm backlog:check`) are scattered across ADRs and rules. The owner, 2026-10-28, on why S60's grader ran against S83's work: *"Any given story may perform work whose requirements are specific to that story's functionality, but another story might do work that directly conflicts. … We'd only want to check each test's outcomes within the scope of its work, not in the context of the entire system."* and on the model: *"The first would be an "at commit time" check which essentially proves the work is complete as defined at the time it's built, and would only be expected to succeed against the codebase as of the time of its commit. The second would be more of a law, something that defines things which ought to be checked henceforth."* Then: *"I think part of this two-tiered model change needs to be a review of all existing stories' verification passes to sort out any laws from the pinned graders. And lastly, some of the graders were modified after the original commit of the story, and I want to either reverse those changes to match the pattern as if it had existed already OR record something that notes why we did NOT change them despite the updated system."* Inserted by the owner, 2026-10-28.

**Impact:** Before S84, a check written to prove one finished piece of work could start failing months later because a different, perfectly valid change touched the same file, and nothing said which rules must hold forever. S84 impact: each story's proof stays tied to that story's own work, while a short, named list of permanent rules is checked on every change.

**Acceptance criteria** (checks `scripts/verify-S84.sh` performs)

1. The [Verify-script contract](#verify-script-contract) and backlog rules 7–9 state the two tiers in the owner's words: a **grader** is a commit-time proof of its own story — every "this story didn't change X" check compares the story's own commits (its base to its shipping commit; before the commit, its base to the working tree), never the base to HEAD; a **law** is a check that runs in `pnpm verify` on every commit and is listed in `docs/v3/LAWS.md`; changing or removing a law is an owner decision, recorded like a budget change.
2. `scripts/audit.sh story <ID> --reverify` runs the grader in a temporary git worktree checked out at the story's shipping commit (read from its completion entry in `docs/v3/AUDIT.md`), never at HEAD, and removes the worktree afterwards; a test shows a grader that fails at HEAD but passes at its shipping commit re-verifies as passing.
3. A design-match verdict is recorded on its own — `scripts/audit.sh design <ID> --design-match -` — without running the story's grader; `audit.sh story`'s completion still requires the verdict for a UI story (rule 22). A test shows both.
4. `docs/v3/LAWS.md` lists every law: a short statement, its source (the owner's words, an ADR or a backlog rule), and where it is enforced (the file and the `pnpm verify` step that runs it). Seeded with at least: browser storage only in `web/src/storage/` (`bt/no-browser-storage`), no arithmetic over BT data in views (`bt/no-domain-arithmetic`), every repository labeled in the personal-data manifest, dispatched routes equal `ApiRoutes`, the 200 KB entry budget (`pnpm size`), coverage floors never fall (rule 21), no copy-paste (`pnpm dup:check`), and every UI story carries a design match (rule 22). `pnpm backlog:check` (or a vault test) fails when a listed enforcement file is missing or its step is not in the `verify` chain.
5. **Every existing grader sorted** (owner: *"a review of all existing stories' verification passes to sort out any laws from the pinned graders"*). A table in `docs/v3/LAWS.md` lists every check of every `scripts/verify-S*.sh` as **pinned** or **law**. A check classed as a law is either already enforced in `pnpm verify` (named in its row) or listed as **proposed**, with what would enforce it, for a later story — this story adds no product tests. Every pinned check that compares a path with a fixed base at HEAD (`git diff <BASE> -- …`) is rewritten to compare the story's own commits.
6. **Graders edited after their story shipped** (owner: *"either reverse those changes to match the pattern as if it had existed already OR record something that notes why we did NOT change them"*). For each `scripts/verify-S*.sh` with a commit after its story's completion entry, the table records the commit(s) and either restores the grader to what it would have been under the pinned model, or records why it was left as it is. Edits made before the story's completion (its own checkpoints) are not counted.
7. Every grader re-verifies at its shipping commit: `scripts/audit.sh story S60 --reverify` passes all 11 checks (check 7 included) without weakening any, and a run over every `done` story with a completion entry reports each grader's result at its shipping commit (graders that predate the per-check contract report their exit code).
8. This file’s S84 **Status** (story heading and top table) is `done`.
